Question 1 — Interpretation of Graphs

3 marks. Identify one issue in the source/display that limits interpretation and explain why it matters.

For example: A graph shows average stress scores but no error bars → you cannot see how much scores varied within each group.

Question 2 — Data Analysis

6 marks. Analyse the findings using the data provided and state a supported conclusion.

For example: Students who slept 8 hours scored 82%, compared with 68% after 5 hours → use the difference in the actual data to explain what the findings support.

Question 3 — Research Considerations

6 marks. Apply a research consideration such as credibility, bias or transferability to the qualitative research.

For example: Researchers interview teachers from only one international school → consider whether their experiences would transfer to teachers in very different educational settings.

Question 4 — Synthesis

15 marks. Evaluate the central claim using the sources and your own knowledge.

For example: The claim is “Technology harms attention.” One source supports it, another shows little effect, and psychological research you have learned provides another perspective → combine the evidence to judge how strongly the claim is actually supported.

Compact exam strategy

  1. Q1: identify one issue in the source/display and explain how it limits interpretation.Example: A graph shows group means but no measure of variability → you cannot tell whether scores were tightly clustered or widely spread, limiting how confidently you can interpret the difference.
  2. Q2: use the actual data — identify relevant patterns, comparisons and statistical information provided, then state a supported conclusion.Example: Group A scored 74% compared with 61% for Group B → use those actual values to establish the difference before explaining what the finding supports.
  3. Q3: apply one consideration — credibility, bias or transferability — to the qualitative source, not as a general definition.Example: Interviews were conducted with teachers from only one school → for transferability, explain whether their experiences are likely to apply to teachers in substantially different settings.
  4. Q4: synthesise — combine evidence across the source material with your own psychological knowledge, evaluate how strongly the evidence supports the central claim, then state a supported conclusion.Example: Source A supports the claim that technology reduces attention, Source B finds little effect, and psychological research you know suggests the effect depends on how technology is used → weigh all three together rather than discussing each separately.
  5. Budget the 105 minutes — all four questions are compulsory.
