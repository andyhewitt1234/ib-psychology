Welcome to Health & Well-being

Why do some people develop health problems while others do not?

Our health and well-being are shaped by much more than one cause. Biological processes, thoughts, behaviour, social experiences, culture, stress and our environment can all interact to influence our health.

In this unit, you will explore why health problems develop, how psychologists investigate them, and what can be done to prevent and treat them.

What will we explore?

💙 Major Depressive Disorder — How biological, cognitive, environmental and cultural factors can help explain depression.
⚖️ Risk & protection — Why similar experiences may affect different people in different ways, including the diathesis–stress model.
📱 Problematic social-media use — How social learning, social comparison, FOMO and other factors may influence health and well-being.
🌡️ Stress & health — How psychological and biological responses to stress can affect us.
🩹 Prevention & treatment — How approaches such as CBT, biological treatments and behavioural interventions can improve health.
📈 Evaluating evidence — How psychologists decide whether explanations and treatments are actually supported by research.

Health problems rarely have one simple cause.

Throughout the unit, students should begin thinking about how biological, cognitive, social, cultural and environmental factors can interact to influence health and well-being.

Imagine this…

Two teenagers experience the same stressful life event. One later develops depression, while the other does not.

If they experienced the same stressful event, why might their outcomes be different?

By the end of this unit, you should be able to explain how these different influences interact — and evaluate the evidence psychologists use to understand and improve health.
