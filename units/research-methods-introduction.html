Welcome to Research Methods

How do psychologists actually know what they claim to know?

Psychologists cannot simply rely on opinions or personal experience. They need ways to ask good questions, collect evidence, analyse what they find, and decide what conclusions the evidence really supports.

In this unit, you will learn how psychologists produce and evaluate scientific knowledge.

What will we explore?

🔍 Asking questions — How do psychologists turn ideas into questions that can actually be investigated?
🛠 Choosing methods — Experiments, observations, interviews, case studies, surveys and other ways of studying behaviour.
📈 Designing good research — Variables, sampling, control, bias, validity and reliability.
📊 Making sense of evidence — Quantitative and qualitative data, correlations and statistics.
✅ Evaluating conclusions — What can a study really tell us, and what can it not tell us?
🧬 Research ethics — How can psychologists investigate people and animals responsibly?

Research methods are not just about memorising definitions. They are about learning to think like a researcher.

Throughout the unit, students will repeatedly have to ask: How was this evidence produced, how trustworthy is it, and what can we actually conclude from it?

Imagine this…

A psychologist finds that teenagers who spend more time on social media report higher levels of depression.

Can the psychologist conclude that social media causes depression? Why or why not?

Think about the answer before clicking the button.

By the end of this unit, you should be able to explain exactly why — and judge claims like this for yourself.
