Welcome to Learning & Cognition

How do we learn, remember and think?

Every day you learn new information, remember some of it and forget the rest, make quick judgements and slow decisions. Most of this happens without you noticing — until it goes wrong. You forget something important, make a biased choice, or struggle to apply what you studied to a new situation.

In this unit, you will explore how humans learn, store and retrieve information, and how thinking processes can lead to both brilliance and systematic error.

What will we explore?

⚙ Classical & operant conditioning — How behaviour is shaped by association and consequence.
👤 Social learning theory — How we learn by watching others, not just from our own experience.
🧠 Schemas & dual processing — How mental frameworks and fast thinking shape perception, memory and decisions.
⚠ Cognitive biases — Why intelligent people make systematic errors in judgement and how to recognise them.
📚 Memory systems — How information is stored, encoded and retrieved — and how it can be distorted.
📈 Improving learning — Evidence-based strategies, motivation and effective use of technology.

Learning and cognition are not just about passing exams — they are about understanding how your own mind works, where it fails, and what you can do about it.

Throughout this unit, students should keep asking: How do fast and slow thinking interact to produce both understanding and error?

Imagine this…

You watch a car accident from across the street. An hour later, a lawyer asks you: “How fast were the cars going when they smashed into each other?” You give an estimate. The next day, you are asked again — but this time the word was “contacted.rdquo; You give a different estimate.

Did the question change your memory of the event, or just your estimate? And does it matter?

By the end of this unit, you should be able to explain how humans learn, think and remember — and understand both the power and the limits of your own cognition.
